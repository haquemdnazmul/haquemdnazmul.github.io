Solar Selector Wiring

Build drawings for switching one PV array between a 12 V, 48 V and 72 V charger with six relays, three IRLZ44N MOSFETs and an Arduino Uno. The Arduino measures each battery and moves to the next one when the current battery is full. Three push buttons choose auto, manual or timer mode, three LEDs show which battery is charging, two more show the mode, and a 16x2 display shows what it is doing. Every part has a reference designator, and every relay connection is given by its pin number.

Before buying relays: K1–K6 switch the PV array, so their contacts must be rated for its DC voltage. Most 40 A automotive relays are rated for only 14 V DC. For a 90 V array use relays or contactors rated for at least 110 V DC, or stop the chargers before switching.

Sheet 3 · Battery sensing

Lets the Arduino see each battery's voltage, so it can move the solar power to the next battery when one is full.

How the automatic switching works: the charger stays on one battery until that battery holds its full voltage (14.2 V per 12 V for lead-acid) for 10 minutes. The Arduino then marks it full and switches, break-before-make, to the next battery in the order 12 V → 48 V → 72 V. A full battery is skipped until it drops below its resume voltage (12.4 V per 12 V). When all three are full, the last one stays connected so its charger can float it. Set the full voltage about 0.2 V per 12 V below your charger's absorption setting.

Sheet 5 · Parts on the prototype shield

Hole-by-hole placement on the blue Uno prototype shield. Columns A–N, rows 1–22, read with the RESET button at the top left. Grey bars are the strips already on the board: every hole on one bar is connected. The parts side shows where each part sits. The solder side is the same board flipped over, so left and right swap.

Check before soldering: on a standard Uno, with the shield's text upright, D0 is at the bottom of the right-hand header and A0 is the top pin of the lower left-hand header. If your shield is labelled differently, follow its labels. Also check with a meter that the rails marked GND and 5V really connect to the Arduino GND and 5V pins.
Mode buttons (J5): SW1 AUTO charges each battery until it is full, then moves on. SW2 NEXT switches to manual: each press steps 12 V → 48 V → 72 V → OFF. SW3 TIMER moves to the next battery every 30 minutes; press it again to change the time to 60, 120, 15 or 30 minutes. In timer mode a full battery is skipped. Mount the buttons on the box lid and wire them to J5. The display's first line shows the mode: AUTO, MANUAL or TMR30.
Mode LEDs: LED4 white (D9) is on in AUTO, LED5 red (D8) is on in TIMER, and both are off in MANUAL. They sit in rows 13 and 16, with the short leg in the GND rail (column H).
Charging LEDs: LED1 green = 12 V, LED2 yellow = 48 V, LED3 blue = 72 V. Each LED and its 330 Ω resistor sit on the same Arduino pin as the MOSFET gate (D2, D3, D4), so an LED is on exactly while its battery is charging. No extra pins or code are needed. The long leg (+) goes in column I, and the short leg (flat side of the rim) goes in the GND rail, column H. To mount the LEDs on the box lid instead, run two wires from those holes to each LED.

  1. Solder the flat parts first: R7–R9 and the bare links, then R1–R15 standing, then C1–C3 and LED1–LED3 (long leg in column I).
  2. Make the solder bridges (short silver bars in the drawing). Then the headers J4 and J5, then the screw terminals, and the MOSFETs last.
  3. Run the wires on the solder side. Use thick wire for the black source bus (C9 → H9 → M9 → J2 GND).
  4. Q1–Q3 metal tabs are the drains. They must not touch each other or anything else.

Build and test order

  1. Wire the control side only (section B). Leave the PV array, isolator and chargers disconnected.
  2. Power U1 from the control battery and adjust its output to 5.0 V with a multimeter before connecting the Arduino.
  3. Install the “LiquidCrystal I2C” library (Frank de Brabander) from the Library Manager, then upload solar_selector.ino. The display shows “Solar selector / starting...”. Open the Serial Monitor at 9600 baud with “Newline” selected.
  4. Send 12, 48, 72, 0 (this turns auto mode off). Each pair should click on after a half-second pause. Check continuity between pins 30 and 87 of the selected pair, and that the other pairs read open.
  5. Press each button: AUTO, NEXT (several times) and TIMER. The first line of the display should show the new mode, and the Serial Monitor prints it. The white LED is on in AUTO, the red LED in TIMER, and both are off in MANUAL.
  6. Each time a battery is selected, its LED lights: green 12 V, yellow 48 V, blue 72 V. With 0 all three are off. An LED that stays dark is probably in backwards.
  7. Press the Arduino reset button. No relay should click while it boots. If one does, check the 10 kΩ resistors.
  8. Connect each charger to its own battery first (most MPPT chargers need the battery before PV).
  9. Wire sheet 3. Send ?, compare the printed voltages with a multimeter on each bank, and adjust CAL[] in the sketch.
  10. Wire the PV side with Q0 open: relay pins 87 to the charger PV inputs, then pins 30 to the PV buses.
  11. Send 0, close Q0, then select a charger and confirm it starts charging. Check polarity at each charger before closing Q0.
  12. Send auto. The Arduino picks the first battery that is not full and moves on by itself when it is full.
